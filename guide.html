SOVL Modding Guide

Open the editor

How to build a custom faction, get it into the game, and put it on the Steam Workshop.

A mod adds a faction: its units, their stats and points, the rules they carry, and your own art. Units are assembled from the game's existing catalog of 274 unit properties, plus properties you define yourself out of the effects listed further down.

Getting started

1. Build the faction

Open the mod editor. Everything runs in your browser and your work is kept in the browser's own storage, so you can close the tab and come back to it.

Start at Mod & Faction and fill in a mod id — lower case, no spaces. It is the name of your mod's folder and the prefix on everything inside it, so pick it before you build much.

Then, roughly in this order:

  1. Images — drop in your PNGs. Include one named preview.png; the Workshop needs it.
  2. Sections — how many units of each kind an army may take. Commanders and BattleLine come as standard and cannot be removed.
  3. Units — a unit per entry, with stats, points, art and properties.
  4. Properties — only if the built-in ones do not cover what you want.

The bar along the bottom lists what is still wrong. A mod with problems will not load, so clear it before you export.

2. Get it into the game

Press Export mod.zip. Unzip it into the game's mods folder, so that the folder holding manifest.json sits directly inside mods:

%USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\DalenStudios\SOVL\mods\
└── yourmodid\
    ├── manifest.json
    ├── preview.png
    ├── factions\
    │   └── yourfaction.json
    └── images\
        └── *.png

The quickest way there is Mods → Open folder in the game, which opens that exact directory.

The game cannot import a .zip for you yet — see What is not in yet. Unzip it by hand for now.

3. Load it

In the game: Mods, then turn modding on. Your mod appears in the list.

  • Loaded — its faction is now in the army list editor.
  • (failed) — select it and the panel tells you what is wrong, one line per problem.

Reload picks up changes without restarting, so the loop while you are working is: edit in the browser, export, unzip over the old folder, Reload.

Build a list in the army editor, then play it in a custom game against the AI. That is the only place mods run for now.

4. Publish to the Workshop

Select your mod in the Mods panel and press Publish. The first publish creates a new Workshop item; after that the same button updates it, because the item's id is written into workshop.json in your mod folder. Keep that file — delete it and your next publish makes a duplicate item instead.

Publishing needs all of:

  • the mod loaded without errors,
  • a display name,
  • a preview.png in the mod folder, 1 MB or smaller,
  • the mod installed locally — you cannot republish someone else's Workshop mod.

Subscribers get it automatically. Subscribing or unsubscribing while the game is running works too; the game notices and reloads at the main menu, never mid-battle.

Everything in the mod folder is uploaded, so delete any stray files first — backups, source art, notes.

What is not in yet

An honest list of the edges. Some of these are coming, some are deliberate.

Where mods can be played

Custom game against the AI only. Mod factions show up in the army list editor and in a custom game against the AI, and nowhere else — not multiplayer, ranked, campaign, or grand strategy.

Multiplayer is the one most likely to change. It needs both players to be able to prove they have the same version of the same mod, and that does not exist yet; without it, two players could silently fight different battles. Campaign and grand strategy tie factions to hardcoded progression, which is a much larger job.

Achievements and leaderboards are switched off for the rest of the session once a mod is used in a battle. Restart the game to get them back.

Rules you cannot author

  • No custom activated abilities. Properties you define are passive — always on, or gated by a condition like charging or in combat. You cannot write a rule the player triggers during a battle. Built-in properties with activated abilities still work; you just cannot make new ones.
  • No spells or magic items. You can give a unit a built-in spell list, but not write new spells.
  • No custom ranged attack profiles. You can modify existing ranged attacks with effects, not define a new weapon.
  • Points costs are not checked. The game will happily load a 10-point giant. Cost your own units honestly, or your faction will not be much fun to play against.

Content you cannot add

  • No new model types. You pick from the 14 the game has, which fixes base size, movement and how models rank up.
  • No custom materials or shaders. You pick one of the existing faction material templates.
  • No sound.
  • No translations. Names and descriptions are shown exactly as you type them.

Rough edges

  • The game cannot import a .zip. Unzip into the mods folder by hand. A button for this is coming.
  • One faction per mod in the editor. The format allows up to four; the editor edits one.
  • Commander-on-mount art has one layout — a rider sprite on a steed sprite, nudged by rider offset. Finer control over how riders sit, and how models layer within a rank, is not exposed.

The editor in detail

The buttons along the top

Open mod folder Load a mod you already have installed, to keep editing it. Point it at the folder containing manifest.json. Firefox cannot pick folders — use Open mod.zip there.
Open mod.zip Load a mod from an exported zip. Use this for a mod someone sent you.
Start over Throws away the current draft. There is no undo.
Export mod.zip Downloads the whole mod. A browser can only hand you one file at a time and a mod is many, so a zip is the only way out.

Mod & Faction

Mod id names your folder and prefixes every id inside it, which is what keeps two mods from colliding when a player installs both. Lower case, no spaces.

Display name, author and version are what players see in the Mods panel and on the Workshop.

The faction's slug names its file; its display name is what appears in the faction picker.

Material template decides how your models take faction colouring — pick whichever existing faction looks closest to yours. Faction image is the banner in the faction picker.

Colours are indices into the game's shared 64-colour palette (0–63), used as the faction's default primary, secondary and icon colours.

Images

PNG only, up to 2048px square and 4 MB each. Names must be unique; they are how everything else refers to them.

One must be named preview.png — it is the Workshop thumbnail, and is not usable as unit art.

Draw art top-down. The battlefield camera looks down, and a model has to read well facing any direction; front-facing art looks wrong on the table.

Sections

A section is a row in the army builder — Commanders, BattleLine, and whatever else you add. For each army size (Warband 500pts, Battalion 1000, Legion 1500, Border Patrol 650, Unrestricted) you set the minimum and maximum number of units an army may take from that section. A minimum of 0 makes the section optional.

Commanders and BattleLine are required and cannot be deleted.

Units

Kind is regiment (a block of models) or character (a single commander).

  • Unit id must start with yourmodid:. This is enforced — without the prefix your unit could resolve to a built-in one with the same name.
  • Points is what it costs in a list. Nothing checks this for you.
  • Model type sets base size, movement and ranking behaviour. The editor shows the numbers under the dropdown.
  • Unit type and pattern decide how the unit is treated by rules that care — cavalry, monstrous and so on — and how its models arrange themselves.
  • Section limit cost — whether this unit counts as One, Half or Double against its section's limit. Half lets two of them fill one slot.
  • Is a mount turns the unit into something a commander rides rather than joins.
  • Stats run 0–20.
  • Icon is the heraldry shape on the base: white shape, black outline, transparent background. Portrait is what shows in the army list.

Regiments additionally set models per regiment per army size — tick an army size to allow the unit there at all, then give the minimum and maximum model count.

Characters instead get a retinue: which regiments the commander may join, or which mounts it may ride (tick as a mount). Tick fights alone for a commander that needs no retinue.

Properties

The rules a unit carries. Built-in properties and ones you defined both appear in the list, and the built-in ones show their description once selected.

SelectorWhat it does
builtin The unit always has this property, at no extra cost.
optional The player may buy it when building a list, for extra points.
pickOne The player chooses one from a set, each with its own cost.
spellList, enchantments, banners Opens the game's existing spell, magic item or banner selection for this unit.

Model graphics

The sprites drawn per model on the battlefield. Add several sprites and one is picked at random per model, which is what stops a regiment looking like a photocopy. Up to 8.

Steeds are what make a unit cavalry: add steed sprites and the sprites above ride on top of them. Draw steeds long and narrow — they render wider than they are tall.

Scale multiplies the model's size; 1.85 is what the built-in units use.

Rider offset appears once you add steeds. Positive moves the rider towards the rear — which is what a chariot wants, since the driver does not stand in the middle of it.

Mirror some models flips a random half of them horizontally for variety. Only tick it for symmetrical art; anything with a shield on one arm will look wrong mirrored.

Custom properties

Only worth writing when nothing among the built-in 274 fits.

Id must start with yourmodid:. Applies to decides whether the rule works on each Model individually or on the Unit as a whole.

A property holds up to 8 effects:

Effect
statModifierChange a stat.
movementAdd to movement, or replace it outright.
toHit, damageSaveShift the rolls to hit, or to save.
combatScore Add to the combat result that decides who wins a round.
doubleWoundsDouble the wounds caused.
extraRankAttacksAttack from an extra rank.
rangedAttackBonusModify a ranged attack.
disciplineChange discipline tests.
regenerationRecover wounds.
deploymentChange how the unit deploys.
ignoreDifficultTerrain, ignoreImpassableTerrain Move through terrain.

Each effect has a When, which is what makes a rule interesting rather than a flat bonus:

charging, inCombat, firstRound, flankOrRear, melee, ranged, monstrousTarget, withCommander, targetUnitType, and allOf / anyOf to combine them (nested up to 4 deep).

Leave When empty and the effect is always on.

Collected from decides who the effect lands on — ownerUnit (the unit carrying the property) or opponent (whoever it is fighting). Aura extends it to the unit a commander has joined.
